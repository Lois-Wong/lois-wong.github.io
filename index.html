Lois Wong

AI Librarian, University of Chicago

I’ve had a longstanding obsession with silence—specifically, the idea of silence as a signifier and absence as a parsable signal. In Prince and Smolensky’s Optimality Theory, it is the non-words that reveal the underlying structure of a language. Debussy defines music as the silence between the notes; Cage shows how silence is compositional. Woolf cites unwritten books and Du Bois posits the “eloquent omissions and silences” in sorrow songs as articulations of pain and tragedy.

My work in AI and model evaluation is one instantiation of this obsession. I'm interested in how archival silence and unrepresentative training data surface as algorithmic bias and model error. Practically speaking, I'm experimenting with black-box methods to identify knowledge gaps from a model's outputs and exploring targeted remediation to close them. LMs learn from corpora that disproportionately preserve the profiles and perspectives of those historically afforded an education and platform while underrepresenting others due to factors like data quality and uneven digitization. These absences are not only historical; they are ongoing. People are still missing and being silenced, and building methods to find them is ultimately where I want to take my work.

I have a BA in Linguistics from UC Berkeley and an MSE in Computer Science from Johns Hopkins University, where my research focused on information retrieval, recommender systems, and NLP. Working across big tech, startups, nonprofits, and academia has given me a strong sense of how novel technologies are developed, adopted, and governed across different contexts and priorities. Projects

Symposium - Interdisciplinary Approaches to AI and Model Evaluation

Models long predate AI as a means of sense-making. Every field builds and evaluates them differently. This symposium puts those standards side by side and asks what they mean for evaluating AI systems that make decisions about people. Proceedings will be published in UChicago's institutional repository.Call for proposals to come.

Partner: SJSU AI Center for Civic and Social Good.

20 April 2027 | Regenstein 122, University of Chicago

Community of Practice

Noticing how many institutions are independently solving the same AI adoption and implementation challenges, I co-founded a cross-institutional community of practice with Sharesly Rodriguez (San José State University) and Will Huggins (Stanford). We develop openly licensed resources and infrastructure for collaboration to reduce duplicated effort and increase collective impact.

Gaita: A RAG System for Personalized Computer Science Education

Gaita is a conversational RAG system that generates personalized Computer Science learning pathways from open-access courseware. Inspired by my own transition into tech, Gaita makes CS education accessible to learners from non-technical and non-traditional backgrounds.
